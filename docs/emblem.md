# The card emblem

The card carries an emblem at the head of the alert band. The default is the red cross.

## Why the red cross

A stranger under stress reads a red cross as "medical" faster than any other mark. The Rod of Asclepius is correct and unrestricted, and most people don't recognise it. The Star of Life is the mark of emergency medical services in the United States and is left out.

## The legal position

The red cross on a white ground, together with the red crescent and the red crystal, is protected under the First Geneva Convention of 1949, Articles 44 and 53. Its use is reserved for the medical services of armed forces and for the Red Cross and Red Crescent movement.

In India the Geneva Conventions Act 1960 gives that protection effect. Section 12 prohibits any person from using the emblem, or the words "Red Cross" or "Geneva Cross", for any purpose whatsoever without the approval of the Central Government. Conviction carries a fine and forfeiture of the goods.

The Act was written for battlefield and relief use, but its wording covers any use. The editor states this once beside the emblem setting, and the person making the card chooses.

## What the tool offers

`emblem` in the card state accepts `red-cross` (default), `rod-of-asclepius`, and `none`. The red crescent, red crystal, and Star of Life are not offered.

Whatever the emblem, the alert band text reads EMERGENCY MEDICAL INFORMATION in the side's language, so the words carry the meaning when the symbol is not recognised.

## Artwork

The red cross is drawn in code as five equal squares, which is how the emblem is defined. The Rod of Asclepius is the outline of "Asclepius staff.svg" from Wikimedia Commons, by Lusanaherandraton, released into the public domain, fitted into the same white square.

## The project logo

The project's own logo, for the README and favicon, is a separate asset. It never appears on a card, and it must not resemble any of the emblems above.
